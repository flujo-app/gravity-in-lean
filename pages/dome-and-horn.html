Room 1 · The Dome

Our world is the shadow of a growing ball

The floor is space, drawn as a flat map with home in the middle. Straight up is a direction we can't see: each traveller's own time, their age since leaving.

Everyone leaves home at the same moment, at different speeds. As time passes at home a dome inflates, and everyone flying straight rides on its surface Worked out. Through space and time together, they have all travelled exactly the same distance.

What we see is only the shadow on the floor. Near the top of the dome you barely move on the map and age fast. Near the rim you race across the map and hardly age Measured. Light runs along the rim itself: all shadow, no height.

Each traveller carries a balloon that inflates with their own time, so you can read ages from above too. Press “Our world” to see only what we normally see, then step back out. You're the green one: grab your balloon and slide it over the dome.

Or drag the green balloon: that changes your direction too.

Your equation, as a shape

(distance on the map)² + (age)² = (time at home)². That's your budget equation multiplied by time, and it's why the shape is round. The panel shows it with live numbers.

The round trip

Tick “turn around”. At year 15 your shadow is back home, next to the one who stayed. On the map you're together again. In the hidden direction you aren't: the height gap is how much less you aged. In 1971, atomic clocks flown around the world came back out of step with the ones that stayed, by the predicted amount Measured.

What kind of picture this is

Picture with exact numbers. “Up” isn't a place you could fly to. It's each traveller's age drawn as a height. The usual physics picture puts home time in the extra direction and has a minus sign where this one has a plus. Both give the same numbers.

Room 2 · The Horn

Falling is driving straight ahead on a flared tube

Now keep just one direction of space: height in a tower. Roll the hidden direction, the ball's own time, into a loop, so that one trip around is 3 years on the ball's clock. The distance driven over the surface is time on the floor clock. As on the dome, everything drives at the same speed.

With no gravity the tube is plain. A ball left in mid-air circles at the same height for ever: all time, no space.

Now the measured fact: clocks lower down tick slower Measured. A slow tick takes more floor time, so the loop has to be longer lower down. The tube flares into a horn Worked out. Slide gravity to watch it happen.

On the horn a free ball does nothing special. It drives dead straight, like a toy car with locked steering: both wheels roll the same distance. But on a flared surface, straight ahead doesn't stay level. The path peels away from the circle and spirals down, faster and faster. That is falling.

Holding the ball means steering it the whole time to keep it level. That steady push is its weight. Press “Let go” and “Hold” to feel the difference, then ride along on the ball.

Why “most clock time” isn't arbitrary

Press “Throw it up from the floor” and wait for it to land. The ball and the floor clock have driven exactly the same distance, because everything drives at the same speed. But the ball lands further around the horn: it cut across the narrow part. Further around means more of its own time. A straight line is the shortest way, so no steered path gets further. On the horn, “the path with the most clock time” just means “straight” Worked out.

Check it yourself

Set gravity to 0. The horn becomes a plain tube, the wheels match even while you hold the ball, and nothing falls: weightless. Turn gravity up and hold: now the lower wheel has to roll further than the upper one. That difference is the steering.

What this leaves out

The flare is exact for straight up-and-down motion Worked out. Sideways motion needs more dimensions than fit on a screen. Rolling time into a loop of 3 years is my choice, to make it fit; the flare isn't. And one spot on the horn means “this height, this reading on your own clock”, not one shared moment for everybody.
