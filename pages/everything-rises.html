Everything Rises

You are moving at the speed of light. You always have been. Most of it goes into time. Here, time is a direction you can see: up.

Drag to look around, wheel to pull back a long way. W A S D to push yourself, Space to stop. On a phone: left thumb pushes, right thumb looks.

Getting around

  • Drag to look around. The wheel pulls back or moves in, a long way.
  • W A S D: your engine carries you sideways through space. Let go of the keys and you drift freely.
  • Space brings you to a stop and holds you there. G lets go again, and you fall freely.
  • Keys 1 to 7, or the names at the top, let you ride along with someone else.
  • T plays the world by the clock you are riding instead of the far station's. P holds time still. R starts again.

How to read it

Sideways is where a thing is. Height is how much time it has lived through. Two things meet when one is straight above the other. Each bead on a thread is one second on that thing's own clock. Rings are flashes of light. The net is everything that has never moved. The pale arrow is the you that never set off.

How sure is this

  • Measured: moving clocks run slow, and clocks lower down near a mass run slow (particle rings, navigation satellites, lab clocks raised by a third of a metre). Light bends and is held up near a mass.
  • Worked out: every path, clock rate and bent ring here follows Einstein's equations for one heavy thing that does not spin.
  • Picture: drawing a thing's own time as height is Lewis Epstein's idea. "Everything moves at light speed, only the direction changes" is a way of reading the maths, not a motion anyone measures. The moments are cut the way the far clocks cut them. Someone moving fast would cut them differently, and neither cut is the right one. Only ages compared at the same place are beyond argument.
  • Invented: the engine, the sizes, how deep you can go, two directions of space instead of three, and the squeezing of heights far above and below you.
