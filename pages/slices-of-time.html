The block

A wave is a stack of moments, cut at an angle

Across and up is space: a side view of a room. Into the screen is time. The glass pane is one moment, our “now”, and it slides steadily into the future. This is the usual physics picture of space and time together, with one direction of space left out so it fits.

In quantum theory a particle carries a turning arrow, like the arrows from Lesson 1. For a particle at rest it turns at a steady rate set by its mass, about 10²⁰ turns a second for an electron Worked out. Think of it as an inner clock Picture. Each disc marks one tick: a single moment of the particle's own time.

The flash test

A lamp flashes in the middle of a carriage that travels along with the particle. Light goes at the same speed both ways, for everyone Measured. For the rider both end walls are equally far, so they light up at the same moment. For us the back wall runs into the light and the front wall runs away from it, so the back lights up first. The yellow band joins the two hits: one moment for the rider, a tilted slice for us Worked out. Every disc is parallel to it.

It matches the measurement

Tilt times tick rate gives the spacing of the crests: Planck's constant ÷ (mass × speed), with the slow-clock factor from your budget rule Worked out. That is exactly the wavelength found for electrons in 1927, and since then for neutrons, atoms and molecules of 2,000 atoms Measured.

Crests faster than light?

Yes, and that's allowed. A crest isn't a thing. It's only the line where our pane happens to cut a disc, like the point where two scissor blades cross: it can race along faster than either blade. The particle itself, the bright blob, never beats light Worked out.

Is there really a clock inside?

Open What's solid is the pattern of crests, and only differences between arrows are ever measured. De Broglie believed in a real inner clock. A 2008 experiment with electrons in a crystal saw a hint of one, and a 2013 “clock from one atom's mass” claimed to use it. Both are disputed, and most physicists treat the tick as bookkeeping.

For pilot-wave fans

In de Broglie's and Bohm's theory the particle is a real dot riding this wave. Its rule: move at right angles to the crests, faster where they sit closer. That is what the green dot does here. The standard theory predicts the same measurements without saying the dot is there, and no experiment has told the two apart Open.

What de Broglie actually did

In 1924 he demanded that a particle's inner clock stay in step with a wave travelling along with it, and out came the wavelength. Drawing it as tilted slices of “now” is a later way of showing the same maths Picture. Notice the discs don't sit square on the thread: both lean towards the path of light. That's the minus sign in spacetime's geometry.
